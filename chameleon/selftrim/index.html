Connect your Chameleon Ultra

Plug your CU in to USB, not Bluetooth, and close anything else using it, such as the CU app or the Python CLI. This page needs Chrome or Edge on a computer. Chameleon Ultra only, not Lite.

Set up the slots

Copies one HID Prox, Paradox or ioProx card into four slots: stock, stock + exact bits, self-trim, and self-trim + exact bits. Load the card into any slot with your usual app first. A backup of every slot downloads before anything changes.

Choose the slots and modes
Use Slot Now Mode

Only the slots' LF side changes: an HF card in the same slot is kept. Each slot gets an LF nickname naming its mode, e.g. "Paradox selftrim+x".

Start testing clears the CU's earlier results and event log, so the results come from the next reader alone. Slots and modes are kept.

Test at a reader

  1. Unplug the CU and take it to the reader. Start on the first test slot.
  2. Hold the CU to the reader on each slot, several times. The first time on each slot, hold it for 2 seconds or more: self-trim measures the reader before it sends the card, so its first read is slower.
  3. Note for each slot whether it read, how quickly, and how close the CU had to be.
  4. Test one reader at a time. A self-trim slot learns the reader it meets and starts over at a different one.

The CU keeps its results while it sleeps. Back here, connect again and read them.

About these columns

Fields seen: how many times the slot detected a reader's field arriving. A reader that switches its field off and on again quickly can count once, so read it as "the reader was seen". Reader vs CU: the clock difference self-trim measured and corrects for. It stays blank until self-trim learns a reader. Measurements: how many fields the learned value is based on. Changing a slot's mode or the active slot here takes effect at once.

Firmware

The test firmware is the Paradox pull request, fixes for answering a reader that wakes the CU, and the self-trim research (source). Flashing keeps your slots and saved cards, and clears what self-trim learned. Stock v2.3.0 has no Paradox support. Flash stock back when you're done.

What the modes do
  • stock: as stock firmware. The CU switches a load across its coil, as a real tag does. That can't drift against the reader, but the signal is small.
  • self-trim: the CU also drives its coil, for a stronger signal, and matches that drive to the reader's clock. At the start of each field it measures the reader's carrier and trims to it. It finds readers up to about ±3.5 % off, and keeps what it learned per slot through sleep. Each field's first 0.4 s is the measurement and carries no card.
  • new drive only: the stronger drive without the matching, for comparison. It beats against the reader's clock.
  • + exact bits: stock firmware sends some HID Prox and Paradox bits 4 % short. A Proxmark3 doesn't mind. A stricter reader might. This makes every bit the standard length on average.

At one real HID reader whose clock ran 0.42 % slow, stock read only close in and self-trim read at the edge of the field. If self-trim reads worse than stock at your reader, use stock, and send the report: that's useful too.

Backup and restore

A backup is a file with every slot's LF side: card type, card data, whether it's enabled, its nickname and its mode. Restore puts every slot's LF side back. HF cards are never changed. The file contains your cards' data: keep it somewhere private. Card types this firmware can't read (e.g. Indala) are listed in the file but can't be restored from it.

For developers
  • Command 3120 reads and sets each slot's mode, the fields seen and what self-trim learned. 0xC1 clears the results.
  • Command 3100 at offset 0xFFFE reads the self-trim event log: the last 32 events, kept while the CU sleeps. Offset 0xFFFC clears it.
  • The page's parsing is in selftrim.js. The card data only passes between the CU and the page's memory. The page never shows, logs, stores or sends it, except in a backup file you download.
